Timeless Question

When does insisting on the exact name for a thing begin to leave the people who must still use it without a usable name at all?

Venn Domains

  • Domain A: Precise Naming (the practiced refusal to accept a label that smooths away the joints, the edges, or the actual load the thing must carry)
  • Domain B: Usable Address (the recognition that a name too exact to be spoken, remembered, or shared under ordinary conditions leaves the people who already live with the thing unable to point, coordinate, or repair)

Overlap: The living hinge where accuracy and addressability become two conditions of the same clarity - a naming that stays true because it has not treated precision as if it cost the users nothing.

Color of the Overlap: Soft Graphite Mindset of the Overlap: Sufficient Precision - the practiced capacity to keep the name tight enough that the thing is not misread, without pretending the name can remain private to the person who first saw its full structure.

Anonymous Ballot

Question: When the structure of the thing is still clearer than any shared name, yet the people who must already work with it have begun to invent their own rough labels, which orientation do you trust first?

  • A) Hold the exact name; a usable label that loses the joints is more often convenience than care
  • B) Offer a name that can travel; precision that cannot be spoken by those who must act has already stopped being clarity and become private property
  • C) Name the remaining distinctions that matter, name the people who must still point at the thing, and release only the name that keeps their next step from becoming false
  • D) Other / it depends (explain below)
