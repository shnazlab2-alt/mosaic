Mosaic Daily Tile — 2026-10-06

Timeless Question

When does keeping a promise begin to abandon the person, or the purpose, the promise was meant to serve?

Venn Domains

  • Domain A: Kept Word (the practiced refusal to revise a commitment the moment it becomes costly, inconvenient, or less flattering than it was on the day it was given)
  • Domain B: Living Revision (the recognition that a promise made under one set of facts can start to harm the very person or work it named, if the facts have changed and the word is held as if they had not)

Overlap: The living hinge where honoring the word and revising the word become two conditions of the same fidelity — a promise that remains answerable because it has not been frozen against the person it was for.

Color of the Overlap: Slate Indigo
Mindset of the Overlap: Revisable Fidelity — the practiced capacity to keep a commitment without treating the original sentence as more sacred than the good it was spoken to protect.

Anonymous Ballot

Question: When a promise still binds you, yet the facts under it have shifted enough that keeping the original terms may miss the person or purpose it named, which orientation do you trust first?

  • A) Keep the word as given; a promise that can be rewritten at the first change is no longer a promise
  • B) Revise the terms; a word that will not move when the facts move has already left the person it was meant to serve
  • C) Name the change aloud and renegotiate the promise before either breaking it in silence or keeping it against its purpose
  • D) Other / it depends (explain below)

Optional reflection (anonymous long field):
What recent kept word, or recent revision, left you unsure whether you had honored a promise or merely protected the comfort of not reopening it?
